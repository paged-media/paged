Alpine North

The heaviest route on the rolls and the oldest: four zones, two carriers, and a driver who has never once conceded a bundle to snow. Carried 1,760 copies this year without a single late delivery.

The zone-four leg was resurveyed in June and shortened by nine kilometres, which the fare card will notice next year even if the mountains do not.

Alpine South

The quieter twin: 1,650 copies, one re-route around the April roadworks, and the network's best bundles-per-stop figure for the third year running.

Its zone-three fare rose five cents in autumn; nobody wrote in, which the office chooses to read as understanding.

The pressmark

Every card carries the house pressmark once, and this card carries it for all of them — a replaced element that fragmentation must move whole, never cut:

Forty-eight pixels square, generated by the annual's own tooling, and owed to no one.

Harbour East

The flood route. Lost one bundle to the September water — waste, uncounted — and still closed the year two percent up on carried volume.

The quay-side drop was moved eleven doors inland in October, above the high-water line the harbourmaster now marks in paint rather than in memory.

Harbour West

Shares Quayline's vans with the east run and, by long tradition, none of its excuses. 1,455 copies, dry, on time, every week of the year including the wet ones.

Its zone-one fare is the cheapest on the card, and the route makes a point of keeping it there.

Plateau Ring

Six years on the print rolls and now the network's most-watched graph: the first region whose readers open more digital editions than they take paper copies. The ring still carried 1,000 print copies, and the vans still run full — the plateau reads both ways.

The spur line was added to the ring's schedule in August, folding two runs into one and saving a van, a driver, and an argument.

Upland Loop

The year's new route: first delivery in October, 85 copies and climbing. The loop was drawn on the office wall in chalk before it was drawn anywhere official, and the chalk line is still there, which is how this house marks a route that worked.
