The Year the Presses Ran Long

Twelve months, one flood, three second printings, and the first year since the rebuild in which spoilage held under four percent. The long account of how the counts were made.

January to March

The year opened the way press years usually do: cold plates, a cautious first run, and a circulation ledger that had been ruled but not yet written in. By the end of March the house had set 1,840 print copies and logged 620 first opens of the digital edition — a ratio the board called healthy and the pressroom called a warning shot, and both were right.

What changed in the first quarter was not the volume but the discipline. The dock tally and the run counter were reconciled every week instead of every month, and the difference between them — the honest name for spoilage — stopped being a quarterly surprise and became a Tuesday routine.

A copy is counted when it leaves the dock. Everything else this house believes follows from that sentence.

April to June

The second quarter was the growth quarter: 2,110 copies set and shipped, and the digital edition finding 880 new first opens. The house colour went on a new plate series in May, and the registration held to half a point through the whole run — the kind of figure that reads as luck until it repeats.

It repeated. The June run matched it, and the crew stopped mentioning it, which in a pressroom is the highest compliment a number can be paid.

July to September

Then the coastal routes flooded. Two deliveries ran a week late, one bundle came back water-damaged and went to waste — uncounted, whatever the driver said — and the quarter closed at 1,975 copies, the year's only step backward. The digital edition, which does not mind weather, grew to 1,210 first opens and quietly made the case it had been making all year.

October to December

The fourth quarter answered every question the third had raised. A 2,390-copy run — the largest since the two-press years — went to bed on schedule, on the first attempt, with the plates still warm from the proofing pass. Digital closed at 1,585. The full-year ledger reads 8,315 printed and counted, 4,295 opened and counted, and a spoilage line of 3.7 percent that the whole building regards as the real headline.

Next year gets one more route, one fewer excuse, and the same three counting rules it inherited. The presses are ready. So is the dock.

Route notes

The Alpine routes carried 3,410 print copies, the year's heaviest load, and lost none to weather. Harbour carried 2,905 and lost one bundle to the September flood.

The Plateau — six years on the print rolls — now opens more digital editions than it takes paper copies, the first region to cross that line.

No route was retired this year. One was added: the upland loop, first delivery in October, 85 copies and climbing.
